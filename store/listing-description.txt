Moon Phase shows you tonight's moon at a glance.

Open it and you get the current phase name (New, Waxing Crescent, First
Quarter, and so on), the percentage of the disc that's lit, and a moon
drawn right on the watch — its light and shadow shaped to match the real
terminator for that phase. No image assets: the disc is rendered from the
maths, so it's always exactly right and always crisp.

The phase is computed entirely on the watch using Jean Meeus's astronomical
algorithm (Astronomical Algorithms, 2nd edition, chapter 49), with the full
25-term correction series for the quarter phases. Timing of the primary
phases is typically accurate to within a couple of minutes — far tighter
than anything the display needs. The value is anchored to 9 PM local time,
so it reflects that evening's sky specifically rather than the moment you
happen to glance at it.

There is no network connection and no phone companion. The moon phase is
recalculated every time you open the app, and the middle (select) button
recomputes it on demand. Nothing leaves the watch.

Built for the Pebble Time 2.
